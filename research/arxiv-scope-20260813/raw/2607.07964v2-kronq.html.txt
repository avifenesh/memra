# KronQ: LLM Quantization via Kronecker-Factored Hessian

###### Abstract ######

Post-training quantization (PTQ) is a widely adopted technique for compressing large language models (LLMs) without retraining. Most existing second-order PTQ methods, including GPTQ, construct quantization objectives from input activation statistics, effectively assuming that all output channels contribute equally to the layer-wise reconstruction objective. We propose KronQ, a PTQ framework that challenges this assumption by introducing the gradient covariance into the quantization pipeline. Under the Kronecker-factored Hessian approximation, the quantization loss depends jointly on both the activation and gradient covariances, and KronQ exploits this at two complementary levels. (1) KronQ introduces bidirectional incoherence processing, extending the existing input-side random rotation to the output dimension using the gradient covariance, reducing weight magnitude variance across both input and output dimensions. (2) KronQ derives a new sensitivity metric for inter-layer mixed-precision allocation, driven by the gradient and activation Hessian traces. Notably, in the case of 2-bit weight-only quantization on LLaMA-3-70B, while GPTQ and GPTAQ diverge or produce degenerate quantizations (\>\>2000 perplexity on WikiText-2), KronQ achieves 7.93 perplexity.

1 Introduction
----------

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable performance across a wide range of natural language understanding and generation tasks 
> ([6](#bib.bib1); [48](#bib.bib2); [17](#bib.bib5))

. However, their growing parameter counts, reaching hundreds of billions in recent models, pose significant deployment challenges, as the memory and computational requirements far exceed what is available on commodity and edge hardware. Quantization 
> ([16](#bib.bib6); [33](#bib.bib13))

 provides an effective solution by compressing model weights and activations to low-bit representations, reducing both memory footprint and inference latency. Among quantization techniques, post-training quantization (PTQ) has attracted particular interest due to its practicality. It requires no retraining and operates on a pretrained model using only a small calibration dataset.

PTQ methods for LLMs span several paradigms 
> ([55](#bib.bib16))

: (1) Compensation-based methods apply the Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) principle 
> ([19](#bib.bib4); [29](#bib.bib14))

 layer-by-layer, using the input activation covariance as a proxy Hessian to compensate for rounding error in weight quantization 
> ([12](#bib.bib15); [13](#bib.bib3); [26](#bib.bib38); [32](#bib.bib17); [50](#bib.bib42); [25](#bib.bib24))

. (2) Rotation-based methods apply orthogonal transforms to weight matrices and activations to suppress outliers before quantization 
> ([7](#bib.bib11); [49](#bib.bib19); [3](#bib.bib18); [36](#bib.bib8))

. (3) Salience-based methods identify sensitive weight channels via activation magnitudes and protect them through per-channel scaling 
> ([34](#bib.bib9); [53](#bib.bib10))

. These paradigms are
complementary and are often combined in practice.
In this work, we focus on the compensation-based family, in particular GPTQ 
> ([13](#bib.bib3))

 and its successors, combined with orthogonal transforms from the rotation-based paradigm. These methods have become the dominant framework for LLM weight quantization and are widely integrated into production serving stacks, including HuggingFace Transformers 
> ([52](#bib.bib20))

, GPTQModel 
> ([42](#bib.bib21))

, and vLLM 
> ([28](#bib.bib22))

.

However, most compensation-based methods share a common limitation: the quantization objective is characterized solely through the input activation covariance 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, which captures only the input-side second-order information of the weight space. Under the Kronecker-factored approximation (K-FAC) 
> ([38](#bib.bib23))

, the full weight Hessian factorizes as 𝐇≈𝐇X⊗𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\\approx\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\otimes\\mathbf{H}\_{G}, where 𝐇G=𝔼⁡[𝐠𝐠⊤]\\mathbf{H}\_{G}=\\mathbb{E}[\\mathbf{gg}^{\\top}] is the gradient covariance with gg as the output gradient. Yet compensation-based methods built on the GPTQ-style 
> ([13](#bib.bib3))

 column-wise OBS solver leave 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} unused, implicitly treating all output directions as equally important by assuming 𝐇G=𝐈\\mathbf{H}\_{G}=\\mathbf{I}. As shown in Figure [1](#S1.F1)(a), output channels vary substantially in gradient magnitude, making this a suboptimal approximation.

Diagonal entries vary by orders of magnitude, revealing heterogeneous output-side sensitivity. (b) WikiText-2 perplexity of GPTQ, GPTAQ, and KronQ on LLaMA-2-13B and LLaMA-3-8B across W4/W3/W2 weight-only quantization.

We propose KronQ, a post-training quantization method built on the Kronecker-factored Hessian approximation 𝐇≈𝐇X⊗𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\\approx\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\otimes\\mathbf{H}\_{G}. By incorporating the gradient covariance 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} into the quantization objective, KronQ accounts for output-side Hessian information, a dimension largely overlooked by existing compensation-based methods. Our contributions are as follows.

1. 1.

   Kronecker-Factored Quantization Error. We extend the layer-wise quantization objective to incorporate 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} under the K-FAC approximation and introduce bidirectional incoherence processing. Notably, 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} cancels in the quantization update, preserving the efficiency of the base quantizer.

2. 2.

   Inter-Layer Mixed Precision via Joint Hessian Traces.We derive a sublayer sensitivity metric from tr⁡(𝐇G)⋅tr⁡(𝐇X)\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}) that differentiates sublayers sharing identical input statistics, enabling principled bit-width allocation across sublayers.

3. 3.

   Empirical Validation.We evaluate KronQ on LLaMA-2 and LLaMA-3 from 7B to 70B across weight-only and weight-and-activation settings at W2/W3/W4, achieving consistent state-of-the-art results with the largest gains at 2-bit, as shown in Figure [1](#S1.F1)(b).

2 Related Work
----------

#### Compensation-Based Post-Training Quantization. ####

The Optimal Brain Surgeon (OBS) framework 
> ([19](#bib.bib4); [29](#bib.bib14))

 provides the theoretical basis for Hessian-guided weight perturbation. GPTQ 
> ([13](#bib.bib3))

 scales this to LLMs by performing column-wise OBS updates using the input activation covariance 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} as a proxy Hessian, establishing the de facto standard for one-shot LLM quantization. BoA 
> ([26](#bib.bib38))

 extends GPTQ with attention-aware Hessians that capture inter-layer interactions within the attention module. GPTAQ 
> ([32](#bib.bib17))

 improves upon GPTQ by correcting for input drift caused by prior-layer quantization errors that accumulate during sequential layer-wise quantization, substantially reducing perplexity at ultra-low bit-widths.
Two recent methods go beyond input-only statistics. GuidedQuant 
> ([25](#bib.bib24))

 reweights 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} with per-output-channel end-loss saliency under a block-diagonal Fisher approximation.
YAQA 
> ([50](#bib.bib42))

, the work most closely related to ours, estimates the Fisher of the KL divergence to the original model through two Kronecker-factored sketches: Sketch A power-iterates under a token-independence assumption, while Sketch B is an unbiased single-pass sketch that retains cross-token structure. Both feed a two-sided LDLQ solver. KronQ targets the same curvature but factorizes it under the K-FAC assumptions, and leaves the rounding step exactly as GPTAQ, using 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} only for incoherence processing and mixed-precision allocation.

#### Rotation-Based Post-Training Quantization. ####

QuaRot 
> ([3](#bib.bib18))

 and SpinQuant 
> ([36](#bib.bib8))

 apply orthogonal transforms to weight matrices and activations to suppress outliers, enabling low-bit weight-and-activation quantization. QuIP 
> ([7](#bib.bib11))

 establishes the theoretical foundation, showing that incoherent weight and Hessian matrices yield lower quantization error, and QuIP# 
> ([49](#bib.bib19))

 extends this with the randomized Hadamard transform and lattice-based vector quantization for practical 2-bit quantization. More recent methods explore learnable transformations, including Kronecker-structured affine maps 
> ([47](#bib.bib25))

 and jointly optimized orthogonal and scaling transforms 
> ([21](#bib.bib26))

.

Mixed-Precision Quantization. Mixed-precision methods differ in granularity. Intra-layer methods allocate bit-widths within a layer: SliM-LLM 
> ([22](#bib.bib28))

 uses weight saliency and CMPQ 
> ([8](#bib.bib40))

 uses activation norms. Inter-layer methods assign one bit-width per sublayer: HAWQ-V2 
> ([11](#bib.bib27))

 ranks layers by Hessian traces, while AMQ 
> ([31](#bib.bib43))

, Q-Palette 
> ([30](#bib.bib44))

, and HIGGS 
> ([37](#bib.bib45))

 rely on evolutionary search, a multiple-choice knapsack formulation, and a linearity theorem, respectively.
Differentiating Q, K, and V, which share 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, requires costly search or per-sublayer loss estimation in these methods, whereas the KronQ score tr⁡(𝐇G)⋅tr⁡(𝐇X)\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}) breaks this degeneracy analytically at the cost of a single backward pass.

3 Preliminary
----------

### 3.1 Compensation-based Quantization ###

Let 𝐗∈ℝdin×n\\mathbf{X}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\times n} be a matrix of nn calibration inputs stacked as columns, 𝐖∈ℝdout×din\\mathbf{W}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{in}}} the weight matrix of a linear layer with input dimension dind\_{\\mathrm{in}} and output dimension doutd\_{\\mathrm{out}}, and 𝐖^\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}} its quantized counterpart. The standard layer-wise PTQ objective minimizes the squared output reconstruction error:

|min𝐖^⁡‖𝐖𝐗−𝐖^​𝐗‖F2=tr⁡[(𝐖−𝐖^)​𝐇X​(𝐖−𝐖^)⊤],\\min\_{\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}}\\;\\|\\mathbf{W}\\mathbf{X}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}\\mathbf{X}\\|\_{F}^{2}\\;=\\;\\mathrm{tr}\\!\\left[(\\mathbf{W}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}})\\,\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\,(\\mathbf{W}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}})^{\\top}\\right],|(1)|
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

where 𝐇X=𝐗𝐗⊤∈ℝdin×din\\mathbf{H}\_{X}=\\mathbf{X}\\mathbf{X}^{\\top}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{in}}} is the input activation covariance, which serves as a proxy for the layer-wise Hessian. GPTQ 
> ([13](#bib.bib3))

 solves this objective greedily column by column using the OBS 
> ([19](#bib.bib4))

 update. The compensation applied to the remaining unquantized columns after quantizing column pp is given by

|Δ𝐖:,p+1:=−𝐖:,p−𝐖^:,p[𝐇X−1]p​p⋅[𝐇X−1]p,p+1:,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p+1:}=-\\frac{\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}\_{:,p}}{[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{pp}}\\cdot[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{p,p+1:},|(2)|
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

where [𝐇X−1]p​p[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{pp} is the pp-th diagonal entry of the inverse Hessian and [𝐇X−1]p,p+1:[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{p,p+1:} is its pp-th row restricted to columns p+1p+1 onward. This distributes the quantization error of 𝐖:,p\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p} optimally to the remaining weights. However, the layer-wise framework causes quantization errors to accumulate across layers, as the objective in Equation ([1](#S3.E1)) is evaluated on activations already degraded by prior-layer quantization. GPTAQ 
> ([32](#bib.bib17))

 addresses this by correcting for input drift via asymmetric calibration:

|min𝐖^⁡‖𝐖​𝐗\~−𝐖^​𝐗‖F2=min𝐖^⁡tr⁡[Δ​𝐖​𝐇X​Δ​𝐖⊤+2​𝐖​Δ​𝐗𝐗⊤​Δ​𝐖⊤],\\min\_{\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}}\\;\\|\\mathbf{W}\\widetilde{\\mathbf{X}}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}\\mathbf{X}\\|\_{F}^{2}\\;=\\;\\min\_{\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}}\\mathrm{tr}\\!\\left[\\Delta\\mathbf{W}\\,\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}^{\\top}+2\\mathbf{W}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{X}\\mathbf{X}^{\\top}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}^{\\top}\\right],|(3)|
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

where Δ​𝐖=𝐖−𝐖^\\Delta\\mathbf{W}=\\mathbf{W}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}, 𝐗\\mathbf{X} is the input from the running quantized model, 𝐗\~\\widetilde{\\mathbf{X}} is the corresponding activation of the full-precision model, and Δ​𝐗=𝐗\~−𝐗\\Delta\\mathbf{X}=\\widetilde{\\mathbf{X}}-\\mathbf{X} is the input drift. The term ‖𝐖​Δ​𝐗‖F2\\|\\mathbf{W}\\Delta\\mathbf{X}\\|\_{F}^{2} is constant in 𝐖^\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}} and omitted. Despite this correction, GPTAQ inherits the same proxy Hessian 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, leaving the output-side gradient statistics unaccounted for.

### 3.2 Incoherence Processing ###

Incoherence processing 
> ([7](#bib.bib11))

 is a preprocessing technique that reduces quantization error by spreading weight magnitudes uniformly across all dimensions. Formally, the incoherence of 𝐖\\mathbf{W} and its proxy Hessian 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} are measured by

|μ⁡(𝐖)=dout​din‖𝐖‖F​maxi,j​|Wi​j|,μ⁡(𝐇X)=din⋅maxi,j⁡|Qi​j|,\\mu(\\mathbf{W})=\\frac{\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}d\_{\\mathrm{in}}}}{\\|\\mathbf{W}\\|\_{F}}\\max\_{i,j}|\\textbf{W}\_{ij}|,\\qquad\\mu(\\mathbf{H}\_{X})=\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{in}}}\\cdot\\max\_{i,j}|\\textbf{Q}\_{ij}|,|(4)|
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

where 𝐐\\mathbf{Q} denotes the eigenvector matrix of 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, and lower μ\\mu leads to tighter quantization error bounds. Intuitively, this is achieved by randomizing the spectral directions of 𝐖\\mathbf{W} and 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, so that rounding sensitivity is no longer concentrated along specific coordinate axes. To this end, random orthogonal matrices 𝐔∈ℝdout×dout\\mathbf{U}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{out}}} and 𝐕∈ℝdin×din\\mathbf{V}\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{in}}} are applied:

|𝐖←𝐔𝐖𝐕⊤,𝐇X←𝐕𝐇X​𝐕⊤.\\mathbf{W}\\leftarrow\\mathbf{U}\\mathbf{W}\\mathbf{V}^{\\top},\\quad\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\leftarrow\\mathbf{V}\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\mathbf{V}^{\\top}.|(5)|
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

After quantization, the rotations must be applied online during inference. QuIP 
> ([7](#bib.bib11))

 instantiates 𝐔\\mathbf{U} and 𝐕\\mathbf{V} as Kronecker-structured random orthogonal matrices, introducing Θ⁡(din3/2+dout3/2)\\Theta(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}^{3/2}+d\_{\\mathrm{out}}^{3/2}) inference overhead per layer. QuIP# 
> ([49](#bib.bib19))

 replaces these with randomized Hadamard transforms, reducing this to Θ⁡(din​log⁡din+dout​log⁡dout)\\Theta(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\log d\_{\\mathrm{in}}+d\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\log d\_{\\mathrm{out}}). However, this incoherence process accounts only for input-side Hessian information through 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X}, leaving the output-side entirely unaddressed.

4 Method
----------

### 4.1 Kronecker-Factored Quantization Error ###

The standard layer-wise PTQ objective in Equation ([1](#S3.E1)) uses 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} as a proxy for the full Hessian 𝐇\\mathbf{H}, discarding the output-side statistics entirely. We recover this missing factor via the Kronecker-factored approximation 
> ([38](#bib.bib23); [51](#bib.bib12))

. For a linear layer 𝐲=𝐖𝐱\\mathbf{y}=\\mathbf{W}\\mathbf{x}, the per-sample gradient factorizes as ∂ℒ∂𝐖=𝐠𝐱⊤\\frac{\\partial\\mathcal{L}}{\\partial\\mathbf{W}}=\\mathbf{g}\\mathbf{x}^{\\top}, where 𝐠=∂ℒ/∂𝐲\\mathbf{g}=\\partial\\mathcal{L}/\\partial\\mathbf{y}.
The true Fisher approximation of 𝐇\\mathbf{H}, which samples labels from the model’s own output distribution rather than using the empirical Fisher 
> ([27](#bib.bib29))

, gives:

|𝐇\\displaystyle\\mathbf{H}|\=𝔼𝐱,y∼p(⋅∣𝐱)[vec(∂ℒ∂𝐖)vec(∂ℒ∂𝐖)⊤]=𝔼[(𝐱⊗𝐠)(𝐱⊗𝐠)⊤]=𝔼[𝐱𝐱⊤⊗𝐠𝐠⊤].\\displaystyle\\;=\\;\\mathbb{E}\_{\\mathbf{x},\\,y\\sim p(\\cdot\\mid\\mathbf{x})}\\!\\left[\\mathrm{vec}\\!\\left(\\frac{\\partial\\mathcal{L}}{\\partial\\mathbf{W}}\\right)\\mathrm{vec}\\!\\left(\\frac{\\partial\\mathcal{L}}{\\partial\\mathbf{W}}\\right)^{\\top}\\right]\\;=\\;\\mathbb{E}\\!\\left[(\\mathbf{x}\\otimes\\mathbf{g})(\\mathbf{x}\\otimes\\mathbf{g})^{\\top}\\right]\\;=\\;\\mathbb{E}\\!\\left[\\mathbf{x}\\mathbf{x}^{\\top}\\otimes\\mathbf{g}\\mathbf{g}^{\\top}\\right].|(6)|
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

Since the joint expectation 𝔼⁡[𝐱𝐱⊤⊗𝐠𝐠⊤]∈ℝdout​din×dout​din\\mathbb{E}[\\mathbf{x}\\mathbf{x}^{\\top}\\otimes\\mathbf{g}\\mathbf{g}^{\\top}]\\in\\mathbb{R}^{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{out}}d\_{\\mathrm{in}}} is intractable, we apply the K-FAC independence assumption 𝐱⟂⟂𝐠\\mathbf{x}\\perp\\!\\!\\!\\perp\\mathbf{g}
> ([38](#bib.bib23); [5](#bib.bib30))

, under which the expectation factorizes:

|𝐇≈𝔼⁡[𝐱𝐱⊤]⊗𝔼⁡[𝐠𝐠⊤]=:𝐇X⊗𝐇G,\\mathbf{H}\\;\\approx\\;\\mathbb{E}[\\mathbf{x}\\mathbf{x}^{\\top}]\\otimes\\mathbb{E}[\\mathbf{g}\\mathbf{g}^{\\top}]\\;=:\\;\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\otimes\\mathbf{H}\_{G},|(7)|
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

reducing complexity from 𝒪⁡(din2​dout2)\\mathcal{O}(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}^{2}d\_{\\mathrm{out}}^{2}) to 𝒪⁡(din2+dout2)\\mathcal{O}(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}^{2}+d\_{\\mathrm{out}}^{2}). Substituting ([7](#S4.E7)) into ([1](#S3.E1)) yields the Kronecker-factored quantization objective:

|min𝐖^⁡tr⁡[𝐇G​Δ​𝐖​𝐇X​Δ​𝐖⊤].\\min\_{\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}}\\;\\mathrm{tr}\\!\\left[\\mathbf{H}\_{G}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}\\,\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}^{\\top}\\right].|(8)|
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

Most existing PTQ methods implicitly set 𝐇G=𝐈\\mathbf{H}\_{G}=\\mathbf{I}. However, as shown in Figure [1](#S1.F1)(a), output channels vary substantially in gradient magnitude, making this approximation suboptimal. KronQ retains 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G}, estimated via a single backward pass over the calibration set and stored as a dout×doutd\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{out}} matrix. Building on GPTAQ 
> ([32](#bib.bib17))

, we further correct for input drift by incorporating the asymmetric correction from Equation ([3](#S3.E3)):

|min𝐖^⁡tr⁡[𝐇G​(Δ​𝐖​𝐇X​Δ​𝐖⊤+2​𝐖​Δ​𝐗𝐗⊤​Δ​𝐖⊤)].\\min\_{\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}}\\mathrm{tr}\\!\\left[\\mathbf{H}\_{G}\\!\\left(\\Delta\\mathbf{W}\\,\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}^{\\top}+2\\mathbf{W}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{X}\\mathbf{X}^{\\top}\\,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}^{\\top}\\right)\\right].|(9)|
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---|

The optimal weight update under ([9](#S4.E9)) is characterized in the following proposition.

###### Proposition 1 (KronQ Weight Compensation) ######

Under the column-wise OBS update applied to ([9](#S4.E9)), 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} cancels algebraically, and the weight compensation after quantizing column pp reduces to:

|Δ𝐖:,p+1:=−𝜹p[𝐇X−1]p,p+1:+𝐖:,p⋅[𝐏]p,p+1:,\\Delta\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p+1:}=-\\bm{\\delta}\_{p}\\,[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{p,p+1:}+\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p}\\cdot[\\mathbf{P}]\_{p,p+1:},|(10)|
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----|

where 𝛅p=(𝐖:,p−𝐖^:,p)/[𝐇X−1]p​p\\bm{\\delta}\_{p}=(\\mathbf{W}\_{:,p}-\\widehat{\\mathbf{W}}\_{:,p})/[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}]\_{pp}is the scaled quantization error,𝐏=α⁡((Δ​𝐗𝐗⊤​𝐋)⊙𝐌U)​𝐋⊤\\mathbf{P}=\\alpha\\left((\\Delta\\mathbf{X}\\mathbf{X}^{\\top}\\mathbf{L})\\odot\\mathbf{M}\_{U}\\right)\\mathbf{L}^{\\top}the GPTAQ asymmetric correction with 𝐇X−1=𝐋𝐋⊤\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{-1}=\\mathbf{L}\\mathbf{L}^{\\top},
and 𝐌U\\mathbf{M}\_{U} the strictly upper-triangular mask.

The derivation of Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1) is provided in Appendix [A.1](#A1.SS1). KronQ inherits the computational efficiency of GPTAQ while exploiting 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} for bidirectional incoherence processing and mixed-precision allocation.

### 4.2 Bidirectional Incoherence Processing ###

> [7](#bib.bib11)

 and
> [49](#bib.bib19)

 show that quantization error can be reduced when the proxy Hessian is incoherent—its eigenvectors are not aligned with the coordinate axes—and that 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} is highly coherent in practice, motivating column-side incoherence processing. Under the KronQ objective ([9](#S4.E9)), 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} appears as the complementary output-side Hessian factor. This raises a natural question: *is HG\\mathbf{H}\_{G} also coherent?*

We answer this via the incoherence measure μ⁡(𝐇G)=dout⋅maxi,j⁡|Qi​j|\\mu(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})=\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}}\\cdot\\max\_{i,j}|\\textbf{Q}\_{ij}| from Equation ([4](#S3.E4)). Here, μ/dout→1\\mu/\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}}\\to 1 indicates maximal coherence and μ/dout→0\\mu/\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}}\\to 0 indicates incoherence. Figure [2](#S4.F2)(a) presents μ⁡(𝐇G)/dout\\mu(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})/\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}} before and after incoherence processing across sublayers of LLaMA-2-7B. μ⁡(𝐇G)/dout\\mu(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})/\\sqrt{d\_{\\mathrm{out}}} reaches up to 0.990.99, but it drops below 0.100.10 for all sublayers after incoherence processing, confirming that output-side rotation effectively renders 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} incoherent.
This motivates bidirectional incoherence processing (BiIP). Extending the input-side diagonal rescaling of 
> [7](#bib.bib11)

 to both column and row directions, we apply:

|𝐖←𝐒G𝐖𝐒X,𝐒X=diag([𝐇X]j​j∥𝐖:,j∥2)1/4,𝐒G=diag([𝐇G]i​i∥𝐖i,:∥2)1/4,\\mathbf{W}\\;\\leftarrow\\;\\mathbf{S}\_{G}\\,\\mathbf{W}\\,\\mathbf{S}\_{X},\\qquad\\mathbf{S}\_{X}=\\mathrm{diag}\\!\\left(\\frac{[\\mathbf{H}\_{X}]\_{jj}}{\\|\\mathbf{W}\_{:,j}\\|^{2}}\\right)^{1/4}\\!,\\quad\\mathbf{S}\_{G}=\\mathrm{diag}\\!\\left(\\frac{[\\mathbf{H}\_{G}]\_{ii}}{\\|\\mathbf{W}\_{i,:}\\|^{2}}\\right)^{1/4}\\!,|(11)|
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----|

where the output-side 𝐒G\\mathbf{S}\_{G} term, derived from 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G}, is
novel. ### 4.3 Mixed-Precision Allocation via Joint Hessian Traces ###

Mixed-precision quantization allocates a bit budget across sublayers by ranking them according to a sensitivity metric. Under the second-order approximation, the expected quantization loss for layer ℓ\\ell is proportional to ϵℓ2⋅tr⁡(𝐇(ℓ))\\epsilon\_{\\ell}^{2}\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}^{(\\ell)})
> ([11](#bib.bib27))

, where ϵℓ2\\epsilon\_{\\ell}^{2} denotes the per-element rounding error variance at bit-width bℓb\_{\\ell}. Since the goal is to rank sublayers rather than compute absolute loss values, ϵℓ2\\epsilon\_{\\ell}^{2} is treated as a constant within each layer 
> ([11](#bib.bib27))

, and sensitivity reduces to tr⁡(𝐇(ℓ))\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}^{(\\ell)}). Existing methods approximate 𝐇(ℓ)≈𝐇X(ℓ)\\mathbf{H}^{(\\ell)}\\approx\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(\\ell)}, yielding the activation-only score tr⁡(𝐇X(ℓ))\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(\\ell)}). Under the Kronecker factorization 𝐇≈𝐇X⊗𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\\approx\\mathbf{H}\_{X}\\otimes\\mathbf{H}\_{G}, this extends naturally to both factors:

|𝔼⁡[ℒℓ]∝tr⁡(𝐇G(ℓ))⋅tr⁡(𝐇X(ℓ)).\\mathbb{E}[\\mathcal{L}\_{\\ell}]\\;\\propto\\;\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{G}^{(\\ell)})\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(\\ell)}).|(14)|
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----|

We define the KronQ sensitivity score as sℓ=tr⁡(𝐇G(ℓ))⋅tr⁡(𝐇X(ℓ))s\_{\\ell}=\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{G}^{(\\ell)})\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(\\ell)}), computed post-BiIP, and allocate higher bit-widths to sublayers with larger sℓs\_{\\ell}. Within a transformer attention block, the query (Q), key (K), and value (V) projections share the same input, so 𝐇X(Q)=𝐇X(K)=𝐇X(V)\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(Q)}=\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(K)}=\\mathbf{H}\_{X}^{(V)}: any activation-only metric assigns identical sensitivity to all three. The KronQ score breaks this degeneracy via 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G}, which differs across Q, K, and V as they receive different downstream gradients. As shown in Figure [3](#S4.F3), the sublayer rankings under tr⁡(𝐇G)⋅tr⁡(𝐇X)\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{G})\\cdot\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}) differ substantially from those under tr⁡(𝐇X)\\mathrm{tr}(\\mathbf{H}\_{X}) alone. The KronQ score yields strictly better perplexity–bit-width tradeoffs, as we quantify in Section [5.3](#S5.SS3).

### 5.4 Memory and Latency ###

Beyond calibration cost, we measure the end-to-end inference benefit of quantization in Figure [5](#S5.F5). On the memory side, KronQ reduces peak inference VRAM by 3.53.5–3.9×3.9\\times at W4 and 4.04.0–7.5×7.5\\times at W2 over the bf16 baseline, consistently across model scales. ### 5.5 Analysis ###

Ablation Study.We conduct three ablation studies to isolate the contribution of each component in KronQ, with W2 WikiText-2 perplexity shown in Table [7](#S5.T7).
First, to isolate the contribution of the base quantizer, we replace GPTAQ with a plain GPTQ base. This degrades perplexity from 8.19/6.99/11.92 to 10.18/7.95/14.52 on LLaMA-2-7B/13B and LLaMA-3-8B, confirming that drift correction and BiIP are complementary. Second, we examine the effect of diagonal rescaling (𝐒X\\mathbf{S}\_{X}, 𝐒G\\mathbf{S}\_{G}) in BiIP. Removing the scaling while retaining the Hadamard rotations consistently degrades perplexity across all models, demonstrating that diagonal rescaling is a necessary complement to the orthogonal transforms. Third, we ablate the rotation directions in BiIP. Applying input-side incoherence (𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} only) already reduces perplexity, confirming the importance of suppressing input-side outliers 
> ([7](#bib.bib11))

. Output-side incoherence alone (𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} only) degrades severely on LLaMA-2-7B/13B as input-side outliers remain unaddressed. Combining both directions consistently achieves the lowest perplexity, demonstrating that 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} provides complementary output-side correction that 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} alone cannot capture.

KronQ introduces a moderate latency overhead over GPTAQ (+8–11s/layer across models), attributable to the bidirectional Hadamard rotations in BiIP, which require dense matrix multiplications for both 𝐇X\\mathbf{H}\_{X} and 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G}. This overhead is consistent across model sizes, reflecting that the dominant cost scales with the hidden dimension rather than the total parameter count. For memory, KronQ additionally stores 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} as a full dout×doutd\_{\\text{out}}\\times d\_{\\text{out}} matrix during BiIP preprocessing, causing a temporary memory overhead above GPTAQ. However, since 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} cancels algebraically in the column-wise quantization updates in Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1), it is released upon completion of BiIP, and the memory required for the subsequent quantization loop is identical to GPTAQ. 6 Conclusion
----------

We presented KronQ, a PTQ framework that incorporates the gradient covariance 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} into the quantization pipeline. KronQ exploits 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} for bidirectional incoherence processing and inter-layer mixed-precision allocation, while inheriting the computational efficiency of GPTAQ as 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} cancels algebraically in the quantization updates. Experiments on LLaMA-2 and LLaMA-3 from 7B to 70B show consistent gains across W2/W3/W4, with the largest improvements at 2-bit, where activation-covariance-only methods degrade severely. The limitations of this work are presented in Appendix [F](#A6).

### A.3 Proof of Proposition [2](#Thmproposition2) ###

#### Step 1: loss decomposition. ####

By Theorem [1](#Thmtheorem1) we work with the transformed quantities 𝐇\~X,𝐇\~G\\widetilde{\\mathbf{H}}\_{X},\\widetilde{\\mathbf{H}}\_{G} of Equations ([11](#S4.E11))–([12](#S4.E12)). > ([7](#bib.bib11))

. Hence

|Lq=∑k𝐃k​k𝜼:,k⊤𝐇\~G𝜼:,k.L\_{q}=\\sum\_{k}\\mathbf{D}\_{kk}\\,\\bm{\\eta}\_{:,k}^{\\top}\\,\\widetilde{\\mathbf{H}}\_{G}\\,\\bm{\\eta}\_{:,k}.|(24)|

Under stochastic rounding the residuals of different rows are conditionally independent and mean-zero, since each row’s feedback uses only its own past. Writing σi2=𝔼⁡[ηi​k2]\\sigma\_{i}^{2}=\\mathbb{E}[\\eta\_{ik}^{2}] for the per-row residual variance, constant across steps kk,

|𝔼⁡[Lq]=(∑i[𝐇\~G]i​i​σi2)​tr⁡(𝐃),\\mathbb{E}[L\_{q}]\\;=\\;\\Big(\\sum\_{i}[\\widetilde{\\mathbf{H}}\_{G}]\_{ii}\\,\\sigma\_{i}^{2}\\Big)\\operatorname{tr}(\\mathbf{D}),|(25)|
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----|

so the off-diagonal of 𝐇\~G\\widetilde{\\mathbf{H}}\_{G} does not enter the expected loss.

#### Step 2: optimality of 𝐒G\\mathbf{S}\_{G}. ####

> ([7](#bib.bib11); [49](#bib.bib19))

|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----|

|(∑jhjsj2)​(∑jsj2​aj)≥(∑jhj​aj)2,\\Big(\\sum\_{j}\\frac{h\_{j}}{s\_{j}^{2}}\\Big)\\Big(\\sum\_{j}s\_{j}^{2}a\_{j}\\Big)\\;\\geq\\;\\Big(\\sum\_{j}\\sqrt{h\_{j}\\,a\_{j}}\\Big)^{\\!2},|(27)|

with equality iff sj∝(hj/aj)1/4s\_{j}\\propto(h\_{j}/a\_{j})^{1/4}, the 𝐒G\\mathbf{S}\_{G} of Equation ([11](#S4.E11)) up to a global scale that leaves the product invariant. KronQ thus matches the data-aware allocation quality at roughly one twentieth of the cost.

Appendix F Limitations
----------

KronQ requires a backward pass over the calibration set to estimate the gradient covariance 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} prior to quantization, introducing additional offline computation relative to activation-only methods such as GPTQ and GPTAQ. Furthermore, 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} is a full dout×doutd\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\times d\_{\\mathrm{out}} matrix, incurring additional peak memory during BiIP preprocessing. Nevertheless, once 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} is precomputed and stored, it incurs no additional computation during quantization itself, as 𝐇G\\mathbf{H}\_{G} cancels algebraically in the column-wise updates (Proposition [1](#Thmproposition1)) and is released upon completion of BiIP. During inference, KronQ requires online reversion of the orthogonal transforms 𝐔\\mathbf{U} and 𝐕\\mathbf{V}, introducing Θ⁡(din​log⁡din+dout​log⁡dout)\\Theta(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}\\log d\_{\\mathrm{in}}+d\_{\\mathrm{out}}\\log d\_{\\mathrm{out}}) overhead per layer. This is identical to QuIP# 
> ([49](#bib.bib19))

 and our fused CUDA kernel keeps the per-layer cost close to that of the Θ⁡(din​dout)\\Theta(d\_{\\mathrm{in}}d\_{\\mathrm{out}}) weight matrix-vector multiply alone.

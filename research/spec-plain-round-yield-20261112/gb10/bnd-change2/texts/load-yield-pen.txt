An estuary survey represents a comprehensive scientific undertaking aimed at understanding the complex interplay between freshwater from rivers and saltwater from the ocean. These environments are highly productive yet sensitive ecosystems, making rigorous monitoring essential for conservation and management. Planning such a survey begins with clear objective definition, ranging from assessing macrobenthic biodiversity to measuring eutrophication levels or tracking fish migration patterns. Crucially, the planning phase involves spatial and temporal design; scientists must determine sampling stations stratified across the salinity gradient to capture the spatial heterogeneity inherent in estuarine zones. Temporal frequency is equally vital, requiring regular tides, seasonal cycles, or multi-year trends to be accounted for in the schedule.

Before fieldwork commences, logistical and regulatory hurdles must be cleared. Permits for water sampling and biological extraction are secured, while local communities and stakeholders are engaged to ensure social license to operate. Equipment calibration is rigorous: sensors for salinity, temperature, dissolved oxygen, and turbidity are checked

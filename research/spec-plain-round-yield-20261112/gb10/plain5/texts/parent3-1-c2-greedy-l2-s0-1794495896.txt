# The Comprehensive Manual for Coastal Surveying: Planning, Execution, and Reporting

## Introduction

Coastal surveying is a specialized branch of geodesy and hydrography that deals with the precise measurement and mapping of coastal areas, including the shoreline, intertidal zones, nearshore waters, and the transition from land to sea. Unlike terrestrial surveying, which deals with static objects on solid ground, or deep-water hydrography, which focuses on open ocean depths, coastal surveying operates in a dynamic, high-risk environment where the boundary between land and water is constantly shifting.

The primary objectives of a coastal survey are threefold: to establish accurate geodetic control for navigation safety, to monitor environmental changes such as erosion and accretion, and to provide the foundational data for coastal engineering projects, such as port construction, breakwater design, and flood defense systems. Because the stakes are high—ranging from the safety of vessels to the protection of millions of dollars in infrastructure and human lives—this manual outlines a rigorous, standardized approach to planning, executing, and reporting a coastal survey. This document serves as a definitive guide for surveyors, project managers, and engineers involved in coastal projects.

## Part I: Pre-Survey Planning and Preparation

The success of a coastal survey is determined 80% before the first instrument is set up. The planning phase involves a complex interplay of legal, logistical, technical, and environmental considerations.

### 1.1 Defining the Scope and Objectives

The first step is to clearly define the "Why" and "What" of the survey. The scope must be documented in a formal Statement of Work (SOW). Key questions must be answered:

*   **Geographic Extent:** What are the precise boundaries? Is the survey limited to the immediate shoreline, or does it extend to a specific depth (e.g., -10 meters below Chart Datum)? Does it include offshore structures like jetties or piers?
*   **Vertical Datum:** This is the most critical technical decision. Coastal surveys require a consistent vertical reference. Common datums include:
    *   **Chart Datum (CD):** The lowest astronomical tide, used for navigation to ensure no depth is overestimated.
    *   **Mean Sea Level (MSL):** Used for engineering and environmental studies.
    *   **Local Vertical Datum:** Often tied to a local benchmark for construction.
    *   *Note:* The relationship between these datums must be established through tide gauge data or harmonic analysis.
*   **Horizontal Datum:** The survey must align with the national or regional geodetic reference frame (e.g., WGS84, NAD83, or a local grid). This ensures that the coastal data can be integrated with existing topographic maps and GIS layers.
*   **Accuracy Requirements:** Define the required horizontal and vertical accuracy. For example, a navigation survey might require 1:1000 horizontal accuracy and 0.1m vertical accuracy, while a bathymetric survey for dredging might require 0.05m vertical accuracy.

### 2.2 Legal and Regulatory Compliance

Coastal waters are often subject to multiple jurisdictions. The planning team must identify all relevant authorities:

*   **Maritime Authorities:** In many countries, the Hydrographic Office or Coast Guard must approve survey plans to ensure they do not interfere with shipping lanes or military zones.
*   **Environmental Regulations:** Surveys may disturb marine life (e.g., via sonar noise or vessel wake). Environmental Impact Assessments (EIA) may be required. Permits for working in protected areas (e.g., coral reefs, wetlands) must be secured.
*   **Land Access:** If the survey includes the intertidal zone or landward areas, permission from landowners or local municipalities is required.
*   **Safety Regulations:** Compliance with maritime safety codes (e.g., SOLAS) and local boating laws is mandatory.

### 3.3 Environmental and Logistical Assessment

Coastal environments are hostile to surveyors. A thorough risk assessment is mandatory.

*   **Tidal Analysis:** The surveyor must analyze historical tide data for the specific location. Surveys are often planned around specific tidal windows. For example, low tide is ideal for mapping the intertidal zone, while high tide may be necessary to access deep-water channels.
*   **Weather and Sea State:** Coastal waters are sensitive to wind and swell. The plan must include "weather windows"—periods when sea state is below a certain threshold (e.g., Beaufort scale < 4) to ensure vessel stability and data quality.
*   **Seasonality:** Some coastal areas are inaccessible during storm seasons or breeding seasons for marine mammals. The schedule must account for these natural cycles.

### 4.4 Resource Allocation and Team Composition

A coastal survey team is multidisciplinary. Key roles include:

*   **Chief Surveyor:** Responsible for overall quality control and compliance.
*   **Hydrographer:** Specializes in water depth measurement and tidal analysis.
*   **Geodesist:** Manages the control network and coordinate transformations.
*   **Vessel Captain:** Responsible for safe navigation and vessel operation.
*   **Data Technicians:** Operate the equipment and manage data streams.
*   **Safety Officer:** Monitors compliance with safety protocols.

### 5.5 Equipment Selection and Calibration

The choice of equipment depends on the scale and accuracy requirements.

*   **Positioning Systems:**
    *   **GNSS (Global Navigation Satellite System):** Dual-frequency, multi-constellation receivers (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou) are standard. For high-precision work, Real-Time Kinematic (RTK) or Post-Processed Kinematic (PPK) modes are used.
    *   **Differential GNSS (DGNSS):** Used when a base station is not available, relying on a network of reference stations.
*   **Depth Measurement:**
    *   **Single-Beam Echo Sounder (SBES):** Suitable for narrow channels or low-resolution surveys.
    *   **Multibeam Echo Sounder (MBES):** The industry standard for coastal surveys. It provides high-resolution bathymetric data by emitting a fan of sound waves. The system must be calibrated for sound velocity in water (which varies with temperature, salinity, and pressure).
*   **Total Stations and RTK Rods:** Used for surveying the shoreline, breakwaters, and other fixed structures.
*   **Tide Gauges:** Continuous recording tide gauges must be deployed at the survey site to correlate water levels with the vertical datum.
*   **Calibration:** All equipment must be calibrated before deployment. This includes:
    *   **Sound Velocity Profile (SVP) measurements:** To correct for refraction of sound waves in water.
    *   **Heave, Roll, and Pitch sensors:** To correct for vessel motion.
    *   **GNSS antenna phase center offsets:** To ensure positional accuracy.

## Part II: Execution of the Coastal Survey

The execution phase is where the plan meets reality. It is characterized by precision, adaptability, and strict adherence to safety protocols.

### 6.1 Establishing the Control Network

Before any data collection begins, a geodetic control network must be established. This network serves as the "backbone" of the survey.

*   **Horizontal Control:** A network of GNSS benchmarks is established on stable land and, if possible, on offshore structures (like piers). These points are observed using static GNSS sessions (minimum 24 hours for high precision) or RTK sessions. The coordinates are adjusted to the national datum.
*   **Vertical Control:** Benchmarks are established on the landward side. Their elevations are determined using precise leveling or GNSS height determination. These benchmarks are then tied to the local vertical datum (e.g., MSL or CD) using tide gauge data.
*   **Offshore Control:** If the survey area is far from shore, offshore control points may be established using acoustic ranging or by tying to known offshore structures.

### 7.2 Vessel Preparation and Safety

The survey vessel is the primary platform for data collection.

*   **Vessel Selection:** The vessel must be stable enough to operate the equipment but small enough to navigate shallow waters. A "survey launch" or a small catamaran is often preferred for coastal work.
*   **Safety Equipment:** The vessel must be equipped with life jackets, flares, fire extinguishers, first aid kits, and communication devices (VHF radio, EPIRB).
*   **Motion Compensation:** The vessel’s motion (heave, roll, pitch) must be recorded by a motion reference unit (MRU). This data is crucial for correcting the depth measurements, as the vessel’s movement can introduce significant errors if not accounted for.

### 8.3 Data Collection Strategy

The survey is typically divided into two main components: **Bathymetric Survey** (underwater) and **Topographic/Intertidal Survey** (land and shallow water).

#### 8.3.1 Bathymetric Survey (Multibeam)

*   **Line Planning:** Survey lines are planned to cover the entire area with sufficient overlap. The spacing between lines is determined by the beam width of the MBES and the required accuracy. Typically, 20-30% overlap is recommended to ensure no gaps in data.
*   **Speed and Depth:** The vessel speed is kept constant (usually 2-4 knots) to ensure consistent data quality. The survey is often conducted in "swath" mode, where the vessel moves along a line, and the MBES records a swath of depth data.
*   **Sound Velocity Corrections:** SVP casts are taken at regular intervals (e.g., every 2 hours or every 500 meters) to measure the speed of sound in the water column. These profiles are used to correct the depth data for refraction errors.
*   **Data Quality Monitoring:** The data is monitored in real-time. The operator watches for "noise" (spikes in the data) caused by bubbles, fish, or vessel motion. If data quality drops, the vessel may need to slow down or stop.

#### 8.3.2 Intertidal and Topographic Survey

*   **Low Tide Operations:** When the tide is low, the intertidal zone is exposed. Surveyors use RTK rovers or total stations to map the shoreline, sandbars, and rocks.
*   **High Tide Operations:** When the tide is high, the same areas are submerged. The MBES is used to map these areas.
*   **Tie-In:** The intertidal data must be "tied in" to the bathymetric data. This is done by surveying a series of points in the intertidal zone using both the RTK rover (when exposed) and the MBES (when submerged). The difference in depth between the two measurements allows for the calculation of the tidal correction.

### 9.4 Real-Time Data Management

Data is collected in real-time and stored on the vessel’s computer.

*   **Data Logging:** All GNSS, MBES, SVP, and motion data are logged simultaneously with a common time stamp. This synchronization is critical for post-processing.
*   **Quality Control (QC) Checks:** The Chief Surveyor performs regular QC checks. This includes:
    *   **Overlap Checks:** Comparing depths in overlapping areas to ensure consistency.
    *   **Noise Checks:** Reviewing raw data for spikes or gaps.
    *   **Position Checks:** Verifying that the vessel is on the planned survey line.
*   **Data Backup:** Data is backed up to a secondary hard drive or cloud storage at the end of each day to prevent data loss.

### 10.5 Safety and Emergency Procedures

Safety is paramount.

*   **Daily Briefings:** Before each day’s work, a safety briefing is held. Weather, sea state, and potential hazards are discussed.
*   **Man Overboard (MOB) Procedures:** All crew members are trained in MOB recovery. A dedicated MOB button is installed on the vessel.
*   **Communication:** Regular check-ins with the shore base or maritime authority are maintained.
*   **Emergency Response:** In case of equipment failure, injury, or severe weather, the team must have a pre-planned evacuation route and emergency contact list.

## Part III: Post-Processing and Data Analysis

Once the fieldwork is complete, the raw data is processed into a usable product. This is a highly technical process that requires specialized software and expertise.

### 11.1 Data Pre-Processing

*   **Data Cleaning:** Raw data is cleaned to remove outliers, noise, and gaps. This may involve filtering out spikes caused by bubbles or fish.
*   **Sound Velocity Correction:** The SVP data is applied to the depth data to correct for refraction.
*   **Motion Correction:** The heave, roll, and pitch data is applied to correct for vessel motion.
*   **Tidal Correction:** The tide gauge data is used to correct the depths to the chosen vertical datum (e.g., Chart Datum). This involves harmonic analysis of the tide gauge data to model the tidal constituents.

### 12.2 Bathymetric Data Processing

*   **Point Cloud Generation:** The MBES data is converted into a point cloud, where each point represents a depth measurement with X, Y, and Z coordinates.
*   **Filtering:** The point cloud is filtered to remove noise and outliers. Algorithms such as "spike removal" and "outlier detection" are used.
*   **Grid Generation:** The filtered points are interpolated into a regular grid (e.g., 1m x 1m or 5m x 5m). Interpolation methods include Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW), Kriging, or Spline. The choice of method depends on the terrain complexity.
*   **Contouring:** Depth contours (e.g., every 1 meter) are generated from the grid. These contours are used to create nautical charts.

### 13.3 Topographic and Intertidal Data Processing

*   **Point Cloud Processing:** The RTK and total station data is processed to create a topographic point cloud.
*   **Digital Terrain Model (DTM):** The topographic points are interpolated into a DTM.
*   **Shoreline Extraction:** The shoreline is extracted from the DTM and the bathymetric grid. The shoreline is defined as the intersection of the land and water surfaces.
*   **Feature Extraction:** Man-made features (piers, breakwaters, buildings) and natural features (rocks, reefs) are digitized from the data.

### 14.4 Integration of Land and Water Data

The topographic and bathymetric datasets are integrated into a single, seamless model.

*   **Datum Alignment:** Both datasets are aligned to the same horizontal and vertical datums.
*   **Seam Removal:** The boundary between the land and water data is smoothed to remove any discontinuities.
*   **Quality Assurance (QA):** The integrated model is checked for consistency. Depths in the intertidal zone are compared between the topographic and bathymetric data to ensure they match within the required accuracy.

### 15.5 Accuracy Assessment

The accuracy of the final product is assessed against the requirements defined in the planning phase.

*   **Horizontal Accuracy:** Assessed by comparing the survey points to the control network.
*   **Vertical Accuracy:** Assessed by comparing the survey depths to independent check points (e.g., lead line measurements or independent GNSS observations).
*   **Statistical Analysis:** The errors are analyzed statistically (mean, standard deviation, root mean square error) to determine if the survey meets the required accuracy standards.

## Part IV: Reporting and Deliverables

The final step is to communicate the results to the client and stakeholders. The report is a formal document that documents the methodology, results, and conclusions.

### 16.1 Structure of the Survey Report

A standard coastal survey report includes the following sections:

1.  **Executive Summary:** A high-level overview of the project, key findings, and conclusions.
2.  **Introduction:** Background, objectives, and scope of the survey.
3.  **Methodology:** Detailed description of the equipment, procedures, and data processing methods used.
4.  **Control Network:** Description of the geodetic control points and their coordinates.
5.  **Data Collection:** Description of the fieldwork, including dates, weather conditions, and vessel operations.
6.  **Data Processing:** Description of the post-processing steps, including corrections and interpolation methods.
7.  **Results:** Presentation of the final data, including maps, charts, and tables.
8.  **Accuracy Assessment:** Statistical analysis of the survey accuracy.
9.  **Conclusions and Recommendations:** Summary of findings and recommendations for future work.
10. **Appendices:** Raw data, calibration certificates, tide gauge data, and detailed calculations.

### 17.2 Deliverables

The deliverables are the tangible products of the survey.

*   **Nautical Chart:** A navigational chart showing depths, hazards, and landmarks.
*   **Bathymetric Grid:** A digital grid file (e.g., ASCII, NetCDF) containing depth data.
*   **Topographic Map:** A map showing the land surface, including contours and features.
*   **Point Cloud Data:** Raw and processed point cloud files (e.g., LAS, E57).
*   **Geodatabase:** A GIS database containing all vector features (shoreline, structures, hazards).
*   **Survey Report:** The formal document described above.

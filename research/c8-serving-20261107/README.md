1. Agentic c8 answered HTTP 408 (the 90 s first-token deadline) on main and on the lane's candidate.

## Problem 1: why agentic c8 answers 408 on main

Reproduced: the c8 prime pass answered 2 of 29 turns 408 with the cache on and 1 of 29 with it off
(`runs/phaseR/r1-c8-on`, `r2-c8-off`), with the GPU 99.6% busy. The prefix cache is not the cause. The
`[ttft]` trace splits each first token into worker queue wait, `prime_wait` (tokenized, waiting for its
first prime chunk; the VRAM defer lands here) and `prime` (first to last prime chunk). Two causes,
independent of each other:

1. **Admission starvation.** The VRAM gate requeues a request it cannot fit and then admits every later
   request that does fit. A 17,166-token request is charged 3,074 MB plus a 1,974 MB reserve; free memory
   hovered at 3.4-4.9 GB, so smaller later requests (4.1-4.7 GB each with the reserve) kept getting in. In `r1` it was
   deferred from its arrival to its 408 at 90.0 s while 15 later requests were admitted past it; its prime
   never started (`prime_wait` 79,968 ms). In `r2` a 13,232-token request waited 65.9 s and was answered
   408 the same way.
2. **Round-robin prefill under a deadline.** Every prefilling session advances one 1024-token chunk per
   tick. Once admitted, a 12k-17k prompt shares the device four or five ways for its whole prompt, because
   in this closed loop every finishing peer is replaced at once: 42-68 s of priming at about 1,000 tok/s
   of aggregate prefill (solo c1 measured 1,113 tok/s).

Holding the queue behind every deferred request removed the starvation but not the tail: its slowest
first token was 84.1 s at the 90 s deadline and 95.8 s without it (`runs/phaseH/h1`, `h2`), and with the
seed batching below one run still answered a 408 (`b2`). The single runs (prime pass, 29 turns):

`sched_sim.py` reproduces the tail from first principles. With the same 29 prompts and 8 closed-loop users,
at 900-1000 tok/s aggregate, sharing the device equally leaves 1-5 first tokens past 90 s (max 96-137 s),
while serving prefill oldest-first leaves none (max 64-70 s) at the same total work. It also shows the
cost: the median rises (24-33 s to 42-46 s), because short turns wait behind long ones.

## Problem 2: why the prefix cache costs throughput on the mixed shape

Reproduced (`runs/phaseR/r5-mixed-on`, `r6-mixed-off`): batch 99.4 against 118.5 tok/s, batch TTFT p50
1,304 against 736 ms. The trace puts the gap in `prime_wait`: p50 420 ms with the cache, 4 ms without;
`prime` itself is 557 against 522 ms. Cause: a cold off-grid prompt arms its grid-aligned seed inside the
prompt (memra#602), and such a session was excluded from the concat prime batch for its whole prompt. At
c8 every unique ~100-token prompt therefore primed alone up to its 64-token seed, one solo call per
session per wave, and each waited behind its peers' solo calls; with the cache off the same eight prompts
primed in two concat calls (`[prime-batch] B=6 tokens=551 in 490.0ms`, `B=2 tokens=204 in 209.3ms`).
Memory was not the binding term on this shape: no VRAM defer, and the device free floor was 1.2-1.3 GiB.

## Problem 1b: the decode OOM in the measured pass

The greedy exactness cell (prime pass, then a 256-token measured pass at c8) failed 11 of 58 requests on
main in both runs (`runs/phaseX/x2-base-c8-g1`, `x3-base-c8-g2`): the prime pass's 408s, plus one CUDA
OOM per run that ended all eight in-flight streams `overloaded` after their first tokens. Both times, seed
inserts of 549 MB and 593 MB took driver free from 3.8 GB to 83 MB within 42 s of decode, and the fa
partial pool's next grow (about 71 MB) failed inside the batched decode step:
`[engine-error] class=Overloaded batch step: DriverError(CUDA_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY, "out of memory")`. The
failure lands after the step's first KV append (the step guard reads `Touched`), so the step-OOM retry
door (`MEMRA_BATCH_OOM_RECOVER`) could not have recovered it.

The shipped tree also serves the greedy c8 cell (prime pass, then a 256-token measured pass) with
no failure: `runs/phaseX/x6-cand6-c8-g1` served 58 of 58 where main failed 11 of 58 in each of two runs.
Its device free floor was 2.2-2.4 GB, against main's 83 MB. Its greedy output was byte-identical to
main's on every row both served (prime 27/27, measured 20/20); main against main was 20/20.

## Exactness

- Mixed shape, greedy, every compared row: main cache on against cache off 95/95, seed batching
  against cache off 95/95, seed batching plus the hold against cache off 95/95, seed batching
  against main cache on 95/95 (`harness/exact_cmp.py`).
- Agentic c8, greedy: as above, main against main 20/20 measured rows, main against the candidates
  20/20 and 19/19 measured and 27/27 prime rows. At c>1 greedy text is not guaranteed stable on
  main itself (batch composition can move a near tie; `research/coldhol-20260812/` saw 62 of 550
  differ), so these cells are compared against main's own noise floor, which was zero here.

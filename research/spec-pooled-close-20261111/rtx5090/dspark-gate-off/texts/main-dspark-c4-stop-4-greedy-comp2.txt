The operation of a seed bank is a meticulous process that blends rigorous scientific protocol with careful logistical management, ensuring the long-term viability of genetic diversity. The journey of a seed sample begins with the receipt of accessions, which are then subjected to a strict intake and cataloguing procedure. Each sample is assigned a unique accession number, serving as its permanent identifier within the database. This number links the physical seed packet to a comprehensive digital record that includes the species name, variety, geographic origin, collection date, and the identity of the collector. This metadata is crucial for future researchers, allowing them to trace the genetic lineage and ecological context of the stored material.

Once catalogued, the seeds undergo a critical phase of quality assessment before they are deemed suitable for long-term storage. The primary metric here is germination testing, which determines the physiological vigor of the seeds.

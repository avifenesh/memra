Planning and executing a weather balloon launch is a meticulous process that blends meteorological science with precise logistical coordination. The primary objective is to collect atmospheric data—temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind speed—at various altitudes to improve weather forecasting and climate models. The process begins weeks in advance with careful site selection. Launch sites must be open, free of obstructions like trees or buildings, and ideally located in areas with predictable wind patterns to ensure the balloon’s trajectory remains within a safe, recoverable zone.

Once the site is chosen, the team must monitor weather forecasts closely. A launch is only viable if conditions are stable enough to allow the balloon to ascend smoothly and if the predicted landing site is accessible. On the day of the launch, the team arrives early to set up the payload, which typically includes a radiosonde (a small instrument package) and a GPS tracker.

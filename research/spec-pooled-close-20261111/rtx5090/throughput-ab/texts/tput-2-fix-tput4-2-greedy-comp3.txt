The lighthouse keeper’s log was not merely a record of weather and maintenance; it was the primary legal and operational document of the station, a testament to the solitary vigilance required to guide ships through the treacherous waters. The act of keeping this log was a ritual as strict as the lighting of the lamp itself, governed by rigid protocols that ensured continuity and accountability.

Typically, the log was a bound ledger, often leather-covered to withstand the damp, salt-heavy air of the coast. Entries were made in a clear, consistent hand, usually in ink that was resistant to water and fading. The keeper would record data at specific intervals, often every four hours, aligning with the watch rotations.

The launch of a weather balloon, known in atmospheric science as a radiosonde ascent, is a meticulously choreographed operation that blends precision technology with immediate meteorological awareness. While the event appears casual to an observer—just a drifting orange sphere—behind the scenes, it is the critical backbone of global weather forecasting, providing the vertical profile of the atmosphere that satellites and ground stations cannot catch.

The planning phase begins days in advance with the selection of the payload. Radiosondes are often provided by national meteorological agencies and must be inspected for integrity. The balloon itself, typically made of latex or Mylar, is carefully checked against the predicted wind forecast for every hour of the flight. If the forecast suggests the balloon will drift into restricted airspaces, such as military zones or heavy traffic corridors, the launch may be rescheduled or aborted to ensure aviation safety.

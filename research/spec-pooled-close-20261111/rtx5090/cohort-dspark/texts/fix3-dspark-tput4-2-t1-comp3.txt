The lighthouse keeper’s log served as the definitive historical record of a solitary existence, a meticulous chronicle of duty, nature, and the relentless rhythm of survival. Far from the bustle of coastal towns, the keeper resided in a stone tower that stood as a beacon against the dark, and his log was the only witness to the quiet drama unfolded within its walls. The keeping of this document was not merely a bureaucratic requirement; it was a sacred ritual, a testament to reliability that ensured the safety of countless ships navigating the treacherous waters below.

Typically written in a sturdy, bound ledger with cream or grey pages, the log required daily, and often nightly, entries. The keeper would wake before dawn to inspect the lamp, clean the glass prisms, and trim the wicks or check the electrical circuits.

When a seed arrives at a specialized seed bank, it is treated less as a simple biological object and more as a critical data point in a vast genetic library. The process of cataloguing and testing these seeds is a rigorous, multi-stage affair designed to ensure that the samples remain viable, pure, and identifiable for future generations. The journey begins with sample receipt and validation, where curators verify the provenance of the seed. This involves cross-referencing the donor’s collection records, collection sites, and dates with physical observations of the seed morphology. If the identity is uncertain—perhaps due to a lack of fresh material or visual ambiguity—scientists may employ molecular identification techniques, analyzing DNA to confirm the species match before the sample proceeds further. This step is crucial because mislabeled samples can compromise the entire integrity of the collection, making molecular verification a cornerstone of modern seed banking.

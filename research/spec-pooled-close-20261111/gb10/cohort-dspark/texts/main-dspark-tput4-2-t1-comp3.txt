The lighthouse keeper’s log, often referred to as the "weather book" or the "service log," was the primary historical record and operational heartbeat of maritime outposts during the age of sail. Far more than a simple diary, this bound volume was a sacred duty, maintained with the same reverence as a ship’s chart, documenting the pulse of the light and the temperament of the sea.

The act of keeping the log was a ritual of precision and solitude. Typically executed in large, leather-bound volumes with heavy, cream-colored pages, the entries were made in a small, cramped script to conserve space. The keeper, often isolated for months at a time, recorded data on a strict schedule. Every hour on the hour, the temperature, barometric pressure, wind speed, and visibility conditions were noted.

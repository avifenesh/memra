The launch of a weather balloon, known in atmospheric science as a radiosonde ascent, is a meticulously choreographed operation that blends precision technology with immediate meteorological awareness. While the event appears simple from the ground—largely just releasing a large rubber sphere loaded with instruments—the planning and execution involve strict protocols designed to ensure data integrity and public safety.

The process begins days in advance with logistical planning. Meteorologists select a launch site that is flat, accessible by vehicle, and free of tall obstructions to prevent the hitching of the balloon envelope. Simultaneously, they verify the structural integrity of the balloon, ensuring it is free of tears or weak seams, and package the radiosonde instrument. This compact device contains sensors for temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind speed, along with a GPS transceiver.

At sixty-two, Elias was a man made of driftwood and granite, shaped by the relentless percussion of the Atlantic against the cliffs of Blackwood Rock.

Blackwood Rock was not a place for the faint of heart. It was a jagged tooth of basalt rising from the churning grey maw of the ocean, topped by a lighthouse that stood like a defiant finger pointed at the heavens.

Elias had been the keeper of Blackwood for thirty-two years. He had been a young man when he arrived, full of the restless energy of the mainland, thinking the isolation would be a sanctuary from the noise of a world that moved too fast. He had been wrong. The isolation wasn't a sanctuary; it was a mirror. Without the distraction of society, a man is forced to look at himself, and Elias had found that he liked the silence, even when it screamed.

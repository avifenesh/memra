# A deep split rung on GB10 (2026-11-10)

**Status:** decided. On GB10 the FA-decode split ladder takes 512 keys a split past 16,384 keys of cache with 4 KV heads and past 32,768 with 2; every other head count keeps today's ladder. Every other part keeps today's ladder; the RTX 5090 Laptop and the RTX PRO 6000 were measured and get their rung in a follow-up (memra#1178). No flag. Receipts: [`research/gb10-split-rung-20261110/`](../../research/gb10-split-rung-20261110/README.md).

## What changes for a user

- **The summation order of attention at depth, for every model with 4 or 2 KV heads.** The rung keys on the KV head count of the full-attention layers, not on the model. On GB10 a model with 4 KV heads (Qwen3.8-27B, 24 over 4; Qwen3.5-9B, 16 over 4) has every cache past 16,384 keys split into 512-key partitions where it was split into 128-key ones; the floor sits where the 128 rung started, so the whole 128 rung is gone on that shape. With 2 KV heads (Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B, 16 over 2) the change starts at 32,768 keys. Every other head count (1, 3, more than 4) keeps today's ladder: those are the counts no timing row or kernel-check shape measured. Gated here at model level: Qwen3.8-27B and Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B (the whole battery on both sides of each floor), and Qwen3.5-9B, which is not on the roster (the calibrated margin gate on real text across its 16,384 floor, eager and batch: no flip, prime and served MATCH). Every other model with 4 or 2 KV heads is covered at the shape level only: kernel-check's bit identity of every split path on both sides of every edge, its dc-vs-eager cells and FA-REF (the split paths at rounding error from an unsplit f32 reference) on seven shapes from 4/4 to 28/4 and 16/2, which do not depend on the model; those shapes cover both head counts the rung applies to, so it has no unmeasured head count. The combine then adds fewer, longer partial sums. Each attention output past the floor differs from 0.140.0's in floating-point rounding, so a greedy answer whose context passes the floor may differ from 0.140.0's in a few words where two tokens are nearly tied. Measured served example: at 260k one near-tie paraphrase on the same prompt ("combinations in a repeating order" against "sequences in a repeating pattern", at character 317), after which the answer is 134 tokens where main's is 120; each arm writes its one text in all three cells. At 131k, 32k and 12k every cell of both arms writes the same text.
- **How large a rounding move can get.** The calibrated argmax gate on a synthetic 16,390-token record document (its window straddling the 27B's floor on GB10) shows the worst case found. At row 16,384, the first row on the rung, the decode margin moves by rounding (13.234 to 13.238). On the next row, 16,385, the eager decode's decision moves by about 10 logits: main's eager decode picks ' ' at margin 7.16, the candidate's picks '6' (the true next token there) at 2.48. The mechanism, as we read it: the decode writes each row's K and V into the q8_0 / q5_1 cache, so a rounding move in a row's hidden state can cross a quantization step in a later layer's cached values; at a position whose margin already swings by 5 to 7 logits between programs on main (prefill over f32 K/V 14.39, prime over the quantized cache 9.89, main's eager decode 7.16, main's batch decode 10.35), that crossing is enough to move the decision. The attention at the floor is at rounding error from an unsplit f32 reference (kernel-check FA-REF), and the gate passes (the flip sits inside its margin budget). The pre-existing gap is main's, on that document: memra#1175. On Ornith's 32,774-token prompt (floor 32,768) main's batch decode already has the window's largest prefill-to-decode spread at the first two rows past the floor (1.40 and 1.22, against 0.21 to 0.85 before them); the rung's first step moves the decode margin there by 0.03 and 0.04 and those spreads by about 0.2 (1.62, 1.41), and the rows below the floor are bit-identical to main's.
- **The draft's proposals.** The MTP draft sizes its attention at the session's capacity, not at the live depth: the prompt plus `max_tokens` plus 8 when the request sets `max_tokens`, else the server's context (`MEMRA_CTX`; open-output charging can lower it). A session whose capacity passes the floor drafts with the deep split at every depth, short prompts included, so two requests that differ only in `max_tokens` can draft with different splits. Their proposals can differ on a near tie, which changes acceptance, never the text: the verify decides every token. In the served cells the tokens per round are 2.64 against 2.57 at 131k and equal at 32k and 12k; at 260k the two arms write different texts, so their rounds do not compare.
- **Nothing else.** Below the floor the target's every split, partial and output is the same as before. Speculative decode stays bit-identical to plain decode, and a warm turn stays byte-equal to its cold twin, because every path takes its split from the same ladder (the one-partition law).
- **Speed.** One attention call at 512 keys takes 0.64 to 0.94 of its time at 128 past the floors on the roster's two shapes. A served speculative round of Qwen3.8-27B on GB10: -7.4% at 131k, -11.4% at 260k, -2.1% at 32k (every T cell under every B cell), flat at 12k (below the floor) and at 1k; TTFT does not move.

## Question

`fa_split_keys` gave 128 keys a split past 16,384 keys on every part. At depth the partials are an attention call's bill again: a 131k cache at 128 keys a split has 1,024 splits, and a four-row verify writes, and its combine reads, 101 MB of partials against 243 MB of KV (`research/gb10-decode-20261110/`). A probe through `MEMRA_FA_SPLIT=512` cut a served round on GB10 by 8.1% at 131k and 11.5% at 260k, but it moves every depth. Where should a bigger split start, how big should it be, and on which parts?

## Decision

- **GB10: S = 512, one rung.** Floor 16,384 keys with 4 KV heads, 32,768 with 2. Only those head counts: every timing row and kernel-check shape has 4 or 2 KV heads, and with 2 a 512-key split already loses below 32k for too few blocks, so 1 head at 32,768 would be a guess. 1, 3 and more than 4 keep today's ladder.
- **Keyed on GB10 only** (`fa_deep_part`): the unified-memory 120a part with 48 SMs, the part it was measured and gated on (the other GB10 defaults key the unified-memory 120a family; the SM count keeps a future unified part off a rung nobody measured on it). The physics is grid fill (a bigger split means fewer blocks, which a 48-SM part fills sooner), but an SM-count range would have changed the numeric class of parts nobody gated: L4 and the small Blackwell cards, H100 PCIe, A100, the RTX 5080.
- **The discrete cards keep today's ladder** and get their rung in one follow-up, memra#1178:
  - **RTX 5090 Laptop:** the timing rows point to 256 keys past 65,536 (4 KV heads) and 32,768 (2), but the calibrated argmax gate cannot run at a 65k floor on the 24 GB card (its prefill reference is one monolithic forward over f32 K/V, which does not fit beside the model: a captured `CUDA_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY`), and the 256-against-512 choice rests on three runs of noisy rows. A numeric-class default whose gate cannot run is not shipped. The card serves the 27B up to about 93k, so the rung would touch 65k to 93k there.
  - **RTX PRO 6000:** one probe points to 512 keys from 65,536 for 4 KV heads, on two runs of 512 (one short of the rule) with its neighbour cards under load in the later runs; and the big-rig ladder has other copies to change with it (the `fa_decode_dcw_rows` literal and the host mirrors in `hybrid_forward.rs`).
- **Floors are powers of two**, so the captured verify's power-of-two rung never straddles an edge.
- **`MEMRA_FA_SPLIT` stays the probe door** it was: it forces one size at every depth. No switch for the rung.

## Which binary the gates ran on

The code head is c73c73eefd on main 031f146690. Against 9592c2dceb (the lever on main 42bd4b07e4, archived on `bank/gb10-split-rung-20261110/9592c2dceb`) it adds, besides main's own diff: kernel-check's FA-DEEP-RUNG line; two reworded comments and the FLAGS row; the rung's head counts narrowed to exactly 4 and 2 (it also matched 3 and 1); and split checks and the decode graph's segment fingerprint that read each layer geometry's KV head count. On the gated models none of it moves a value: their full-attention layers have one geometry each, equal to the config's (4 KV heads on all of the 27B's and the 9B's, 2 on all of Ornith's, the MTP layers included), so the rung, the checks and the fingerprint give them what they gave before. At 9592c2dceb, on GB10: the full kernel-check (unit 1); run-spec K=1..8 at both straddle prompts, the six warm == cold pairs, the five argmax margin arms and spec-serve-gate (unit 2, the binaries copied from unit 1 with sha256 checked); the served B/T at 131k and 260k. At 764cd39b5b, the previous code head: the pairs again (main 031f146690 changed the server's worker), run-spec, the full kernel-check on GB10 and the FAST kernel-check (`MEMRA_KC_FAST=1`) on the RTX 5090, Qwen3.5-9B's margin arms and the B/T at 32k and 12k. At c73c73eefd: the GB10 kernel-check (its binary byte-identical to bf25fcb629's, which ran ALL GREEN), the 5090's FAST kernel-check, run-spec at both straddle prompts and the six pairs, and the CPU suites. The segment fingerprint keeps the config's geometry as its first entry, so decode graphs can only be cut into more segments, never fewer; of the loadable architectures only Step-3.7 has more than one full-attention geometry (MiMoV2's table has two but its load refuses), and it never captures a decode graph. Main 031f146690's engine-side changes (an env-audit refusal entry, an HF vision config spelling, doc comments and tests) do not reach the engine gates. The margin arms first ran at 1a4f769861 (the first rung commit before the rebase, on main 6cffb35360) against main 6cffb35360's binary; their reruns at 9592c2dceb on the other unit are bit-identical in every window row and verdict line. They stand for the head for two reasons:

- **The candidate side.** GB10's ladder is the same at both commits: 1a4f769861 keys the rung on parts under 64 SMs, the head on `fa_deep_part` (the unified-memory 120a part with 48 SMs), with the same values for 4 and 2 KV heads (512 keys past 16,384 and past 32,768) and the same ladder below; GB10's 48 SMs take the same arm. The head's kernel-check shows the same edges, and the head's reruns of the margin cells are compared row by row in the research README.
- **The main side.** Main's commits from 6cffb35360 to 42bd4b07e4 change, under `crates/`, `memra-engine/src/{spec.rs, dflash.rs, lib.rs}`, `memra-engine/cu/spec_sample.cu`, `memra-engine/src/bin/sample_check.rs`, `memra-sampling/src/lib.rs` and `memra-server/src/worker.rs`. The probe runs `forward_last` and `prime_cache` (`hybrid_forward.rs`), `decode_step` (`decode.rs`) and `decode_step_batch_sampled_lean_masked` (`decode_batch.rs`) at temperature 0; none of those files changed. They reach the changed code only through the Gumbel perturbation (`lib.rs`'s `gumbel_perturb_*` and `spec_sample.cu`'s three `gumbel_perturb_*_f32` kernels), which runs only at `temp > 0` (`decode_batch.rs` takes `argmax_token_device_col` first at `temp <= 0`), and the margins are read from the logits before sampling. The rest of the diff is the spec session and draft capture (`spec.rs`), the DFlash drafter's ring (`dflash.rs`), a doc comment (`memra-sampling`), another binary and the server crate. The `spec.rs` helpers the target path calls have the same text at both commits.

## Rejected

- **GB10 at 256 keys.** Faster than 128 at every depth from 16k on both shapes, and level with 512 at 32k on 24/4 (512 leads by 4 to 10 points on 16/2), but from 65k it gets 0.62 to 0.89 of 512's gain on every launch of both shapes (131k, 24/4: rows walk 0.87 against 0.81, one row 0.80 against 0.73). With 2 KV heads it wins a mid band where 512 loses (17k: 0.89 to 0.96 of 128 on all four launches, one run); that is 4 to 11% of a small part of the round, not a second rung.
- **GB10 at 1,024 keys.** Against 512: at 131k 1 to 4% faster on 24/4, from 2% faster to 3% slower on 16/2; at 262k 2 to 3% faster on 24/4 and 4 to 8% on 16/2; slower at 32k to 49k (rows walk 1.07 to 1.38 of 512 on both shapes). For one row it is slower than 128 at 16k to 17k (1.18 to 1.30 on 24/4, 1.50 to 1.61 on 16/2) and on 16/2 up to 32k (1.06 to 1.48). The design's served probe measured it 1.3% and 1.5% a round faster than 512 at 131k and 260k: not enough for a second rung.
- **A rung on the RTX 5090 Laptop in this change** (256 keys past 65,536 and 32,768, built and gated as far as the card allows): kernel-check's rung-edge and dc-vs-eager cells were bitdiff 0 there and run-spec K=1..8 passed across 65,536, but the margin gate could not run at the floor. Moved to memra#1178.
- **One floor for every KV head count.** On GB10 past 16,384 a 512-key split gives a 2-KV-head model about 66 blocks for one row on 48 SMs, and one row is 10 to 18% slower than at 128 keys until 32k. A floor at 32,768 for both gives up the 27B's 6 to 21% per call between 16k and 32k.
- **The design note's 65,536 floor on GB10.** Every launch is already faster from 16k (4 KV heads) or 32k (2).
- **An SM-count range for the key.** It would move unmeasured parts.
- **A switch for the rung.** A measured default is the only path; `MEMRA_FA_SPLIT` reproduces any arm for a probe.
